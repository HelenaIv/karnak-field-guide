# Close-observation content standard

Owner direction, 2026-11-13: this must not become a shallow monument walkthrough. Detail entries are the core product; place summaries and routes are context.

Each developed detail should begin with a concrete question a curious visitor could ask while inspecting something: Why does this sign sit below the stone surface? Why are these capitals open? Why does the dead king hold an object normally held by a god?

A complete entry needs:

1. A reliable physical anchor: monument, wall/face/column, scene/register and edition identifier where established. If unknown, say so; do not fabricate a map pin.
2. A simple visual distinction to inspect, linked to a rights-cleared photograph or original explanatory diagram.
3. An explanation of how it works, what it means in this particular context, and why it matters. Separate technique, interpretation and royal rhetoric.
4. A small exercise: compare edges, follow a load path, identify separate caption zones, or notice evidence of modification. Never direct touching, rubbing or scraping.
5. Claim-level citations and limits; at least one exact edition check for a translated inscription.
6. A useful connection to a different detail, building phase, ruler, ritual or place.

For sign lessons, the finished unit should show the actual, verified example; identify direction and grouping; explain phonetic/semantic function in that word; offer a short reading with a source; and distinguish literal translation from conventional Egyptological rendering. A sign's pictorial appearance does not imply a single fixed meaning in every inscription. No general “symbol dictionary” substituted for reading a real wall.

## Planned detail series

| Series | Questions | Evidence still required |
|---|---|---|
| Stone technique | Raised versus sunk? Why conversion? What does recutting leave? | Matched specific panels, OIP commentary, usable photos |
| Wall grammar | Who acts? Which text names the action? Who is speaking? | One labelled scene with exact edition match |
| Royal names | Two cartouches? Different spellings? Builder or later claimant? | Paired names, transliteration, documented recutting |
| Colour | What was painted but never carved? | Numbered bark scene and high-resolution image |
| Structural design | Why tall central columns? How did stone roofing work? | Clerestory image, original labelled section |
| Ritual space | Why portable boats and multiple shrines? | Specific scene and shrine-function evidence |
| Small divine attributes | Why does a deified king hold the life sign? | South-wall scene/plate match |
| Building archaeology | Why are earlier chapels inside a later pylon? | Reused-block documentation and museum display linkage |

The current corpus starts these series but does not claim they are all finished. The inscription dossier explicitly distinguishes a researched question from an edition-verified reading. Animations are optional explanations, never replacements for documentary evidence.
