# Source review — 2026-11-13

## Consultation scope

Read the Digital Karnak monument pages registered as `dk-*`, plus the Memphis project pages registered as `memphis-*`. Sources are in `content/sources.json`; claim arrays include named section/caption locators. These are institutional research syntheses, not substitutes for full epigraphic editions. The source accounts often describe a reconstruction model as well as the surviving building; those must remain distinct.

Read the publisher catalogue for OIP 142: verified authors, year, scope, DOI and table-of-contents plate ranges. **Did not yet inspect the full plate/commentary pair for an individual inscription.** The Sety life-sign dossier therefore withholds transcription and translation. A filename suggesting plate 48 is a research lead, not a checked plate citation.

Digital Karnak was launched at UCLA in 2008 and moved to UCSC in 2021, as stated on its home page. Use the current UCSC pages instead of dead UCLA links. Do not treat a site's current footer year as the date its archaeological interpretation was written.

## Source defects and limits

- Memphis, *About the Architecture*: the displayed Amenhotep III reign dates contain an apparent typo. Do not copy them. Use chronology-specific estimates after independent review.
- Memphis, *Interior Wall Scenes*: a paragraph about Sety's completion repeats “south wing” where the narrative is internally inconsistent. Do not derive a construction-phase map from that sentence. The concluding absolute statement about royal descent is also too categorical for guide copy.
- Digital Karnak, *4th Pylon and Enclosure*, “Thutmose I” phase paragraph repeats text about Sety I and the Third Pylon. Use only the monument introduction for the new Fourth Pylon seed; corroborate against publications before extending phases.
- Digital Karnak, *Khonsu Temple*: phase heading says Ramesses II while the body identifies Ramesses III; its list also includes “Ramesses XII”. Do not propagate these labels. Detailed construction versus decoration remains disputed.
- Digital Karnak, *Opet Temple*: explicitly acknowledges simplified reconstruction and uncertain phase attributions; a paragraph also refers to Khonsu within the Opet account. Treat the model as an older hypothesis, not current proof.
- eScholarship item 1f28q08h (Karnak: Development of the Temple of Amun-Ra) returned a verification wall; no full-text claims taken from it.
- CFEETK archives page returned 502 during direct web consultation. Search results located it, but this is not a completed archive consultation. No new exact KIU identifiers claimed.

## Subsequent deeper consultation

Full PDF access to Sullivan (2010) succeeded through the direct eScholarship content URL. Read origins/Middle Kingdom and Akhmenu sections. Earlier verification-wall failure is historical, not the final access state.

OIP 142 part 2 obtained from Zenodo record 18944602, cross-checked against the official catalogue title/authors. Read title page and selected passages: pp. 18–19 (§2.3.2) and pp. 98–100 (plates 48–49). The edition treats sunk relief as more than a production shortcut. Plate 48 commentary establishes B92's location and evidence of conversion; it does not by itself verify a sign-by-sign transcription. Official part-3 download links were found, but fetching the PDF returned 403; no part-3 inspection is claimed. A project reference thumbnail was inspected temporarily, not imported or treated as commercially reusable evidence.

Brooklyn 2016 Mut report: read abstract and pp. 1–3 on paving and later occupation west of the Taharqa Gate. ARCE expedition account supplied broader precinct context. Louvre E 13481 BIS catalogue supplied provenance/current collection context for the Chamber of Ancestors. UCL Digital Egypt supplied general direction/orientation conventions; these are not exact Karnak caption translations.

Further Digital Karnak pages read: Fifth through Tenth Pylons, Wadjet Hall, Middle Kingdom Court, Aten Temples, Bab el-Amara, Hakoris chapel, Contra Temple, Osiris catacombs, Amenhotep I chapels, Thutmose IV peristyle and Akhmenu.
